How to read this model

Each arrow points toward the proteins producing that osmotic pull. Arrow length and thickness increase with oncotic pressure. After reveal, the red arrow shows net oncotic pressure (right pull minus left pull); at zero net pressure, its length is zero. It updates as water redistributes. The opposing hydrostatic pressure is shown separately below. At equal concentrations, both arrows are 30 mmHg and there is no net water movement, although water still exchanges in both directions.

This is a qualitative teaching model: π = 3 × protein concentration (%) in mmHg, calibrated to your requested 10% → 30 mmHg. This linear relationship is illustrative, not a clinical protein–pressure equation. Protein percentage represents concentration (not particle count); molecule symbols are schematic.

After reveal, protein amount remains fixed on each side and total water volume is conserved. Water moves toward greater oncotic pressure, diluting that side and concentrating the other. A rising fluid column creates an opposing hydrostatic pressure. Flow stops when the pressure difference balances it. The height scale and time are compressed for teaching; this is not a literal manometer. Changing a slider restarts at equal water levels.
